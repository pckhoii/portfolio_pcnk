# Motion Principles

Motion is evidence of change. It never exists as decoration.

1. **Explain transformation.** A line draws because a relationship is being formed; a marker locks because a decision is being made.
2. **Preserve continuity.** An object evolves, enters from a believable edge, or resolves into a related artifact. Nothing teleports into an unrelated scene.
3. **One primary motion at a time.** A viewport may have one explanatory movement and, only if needed, one very subtle ambient state.
4. **Give objects weight.** Use restrained opacity, line-drawing, alignment, and small position changes. Avoid bounce, perpetual float, rapid rotation, zoom spectacle, and independent particle loops.
5. **Motion confirms interaction.** Hover may reveal a technical detail; focus confirms the target; active controls change the same diagram.
6. **Arrive quietly and leave quietly.** Page transition is a short crossfade. Hero reveal is a single artifact/line resolution. The footer has no flourish.
7. **Respect reduced motion.** With `prefers-reduced-motion`, display resolved graphic states immediately; controls remain fully usable.

No motion may be added until it states: trigger, narrative reason, affected artifact, duration range, reduced-motion fallback, and stop condition.
