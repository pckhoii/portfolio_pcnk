# Art Direction

## The World

The Data Observatory is an instrument room for attention. It is not literal astronomy and it is not a futuristic control deck. Its visual references are scientific field guides, NASA mission documentation, information design, museum labels, and quiet editorial publications.

## Composition

Every route has a calm arrival, one dominant artifact, an ordered content field, and a quiet exit. Use asymmetry for editorial tension, but preserve clear reading order. Large empty areas are deliberate measurements of importance.

## Graphic Grammar

- Draw with thin monoline strokes and a transparent or near-black field.
- Use coordinate axes, markers, arrows, routes, grid fragments, and compact annotations only when they communicate structure.
- Keep illustration abstract. An Observatory is a measurement plate, not a literal building; a Book is a knowledge diagram, not a realistic object.
- Professional diagrams use steel blue/soft cyan. Personal diagrams may add grey lavender. Muted gold marks a point of attention or resolved direction.
- Use paper texture only for personal/archive contexts and keep it nearly imperceptible.

## What Does Not Belong

No planets, galaxies as wallpaper, random stars, generic 3D objects, emoji, clipart, stock scenes, glassmorphism, purple gradients, neon borders, gaming HUDs, rounded SaaS card grids, or giant personal monograms.

## Accessibility

Artifacts are supplementary unless they operate as controls. Every functional diagram has keyboard access, visible focus, text equivalent, and a static reduced-motion state. Text and meaningful routes must retain contrast against all surfaces.
